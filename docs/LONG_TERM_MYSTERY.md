# Long-Term Mystery — *Titik Buta* (Blind Spot)

Status: **working concept.** Only the items marked **[CANON]** are committed,
and those are all small seeds in CASE-001 v2. Everything marked **[WORKING]**
may change after CASE-003 is drafted. Decisions: `DESIGN_DECISIONS.md` D-36 to
D-38.

## The idea in one paragraph

Kota Arwana has cameras everywhere, and a quiet business in making them not
work. Someone sells **blind spots**: hours or nights when a building's, a
street's or a lift's cameras go dark for "scheduled maintenance". The customers
are not a conspiracy. They're ordinary people with something to hide (a partner
emptying a file drawer, a shop owner moving stock) and, increasingly,
professionals. Most cases in a season have nothing to do with it. A few have a
blind spot in the background. Players who notice start a Dossier. At the end
of the season, the Dossier leads to the person selling the dark.

## Why this thread

- **It grows out of CASE-001.**
  - Victor's cameras were dark because he paid for it (v2 canon).
  - That was the case's own logic, so the thread is not bolted on afterwards.
- **It's Malaysian without being about stereotypes.** CCTV is everywhere in
  Malaysian cities (guardhouses, mamak back doors, lifts, dashcams), and so
  are broken cameras and "the contractor will come next week".
- **It fits the detective fantasy.** The city writes everything down; the
  villain sells the pages that go missing. It is observation against erasure.
- **It keeps cases self-contained.** A bought blind spot is incidental to each
  crime, never its solution. Every case is solvable without it.
- **It scales.** The broker has customers across every district, and each one
  can be a different kind of case.

## The pieces

### PANTAU Sekuriti **[CANON: the name and the sticker]** **[WORKING: its role]**
- **Canon:** a camera installation and maintenance contractor. Its sticker
  reads *Servis 24 Jam* on the Wisma Delima monitor.
- **Working:**
  - Legitimate on paper, and most of its staff are.
  - The blind spots are sold through its maintenance tickets: a real ticket
    number, a real "fault", a real four-day wait.
  - The company is the *channel*, not the villain.

### The Technician **[WORKING]**
- The person who sells the dark.
- A former technical officer who helped wire the city's first public camera
  network, and who knows every system's maintenance backdoor.
- **Motive:** money at first; later a kind of contempt for a city that trusts
  cameras more than people. They never commit the crimes they enable.
- **Identity:** **[OPEN]** until the finale is outlined. A working candidate is
  a PANTAU field manager nobody looks at twice.

### Kunang **[WORKING]**
- **Who:** an original thief, a character of our own, not a copy.
  - Kunang only steals from people who bought a blind spot, and only inside
    the blind spot they bought.
  - Each time, Kunang leaves a small **firefly tag** (a glowing sticker) where a
    camera should have been watching.
  - The name is from *kunang-kunang*, the firefly. Fireflies are a light in the
    dark, and the riverside colonies are a real and loved piece of Malaysian
    nature.
- **Why:** Kunang is exposing the market by robbing its customers. They are
  neither ally nor enemy. The SCU is ordered to catch Kunang, and the player may
  come to understand them.
- **Rules for Kunang:**
  - never violent, never kills;
  - no costume spectacle, no magic, no calling-card riddles in advance;
  - a professional who is always one step inside the dark.
- **First appearance:** CASE-003.

### Nora's history **[WORKING]**
- Twelve years ago, as an inspector, Nora worked a robbery where the cameras
  "failed" for four days. It was closed from above before she could follow the
  contractor.
- She sent the new detective to Wisma Delima because the building's cameras
  were PANTAU's, and she didn't tell them, so they'd see it for themselves.
- Revealed late in the season, if kept. It must not make Nora a manipulator the
  player can't trust; it should make her human.

### The Dossier **[WORKING, mechanic]**
- A panel on the SCU case board that collects **optional** thread facts from any
  case: a sticker, a receipt, a firefly tag, a ticket number.
- No case needs it. The finale is fully playable without it.
- A fuller Dossier opens extra routes and a better ending in the finale, using
  the existing epilogue-variant mechanism (P7).

## Rules the thread must obey

1. **Every case is complete and satisfying on its own.** The thread never
   supplies a case's culprit, motive or proof.
2. **Thread evidence is always optional** and never blocks progress.
3. **No world-ending stakes.** It is corrosion, not apocalypse.
4. **No chosen one.** The detective matters because they notice, not because of
   destiny or bloodline.
5. **Real institutions stay out.** The villain works through a fictional
   contractor, not the police or any real body.
6. **Each season resolves something.** The Technician's market ends in Season
   1, even if Kunang stays in the dark.

## Rejected alternatives

| Alternative | Why not |
|---|---|
| A serial killer across cases | It makes every case about one monster, the tone darkens past the audience, and the cases stop feeling different |
| A police corruption conspiracy | Too close to representing a real institution, and a cliché |
| A secret society or cult | Culturally and religiously sensitive in Malaysia, with no gain |
| A tech mega-corporation villain | Generic, and it ignores what's specific about the city |
| No thread at all | Loses the owner's goal that cases can eventually connect |

## What makes it canon

The owner reviews this after CASE-003 is outlined. Until then, only the
CASE-001 v2 seeds are fixed, and they stand alone even if the thread is later
dropped:
- the sticker is flavour;
- the receipt is a believable detail of Victor's fraud.
